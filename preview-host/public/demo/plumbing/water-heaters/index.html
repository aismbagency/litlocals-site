First split

Is it leaking, or is it just cold?

A dry tank that will not light, a breaker that tripped, a pilot that will not hold — that can wait for the desk if the house is otherwise fine. A tank that is weeping, rusting at the base, or hissing onto a finished floor is closer to the emergency page.

Oak & Main does not claim a brand, a rating, or a license. A live shop writes the units they actually set, and the lead time they actually have. The page’s job is to make the homeowner say the useful sentence: “no hot water” or “the tank is wet.”

Second split

Repair, or the tank is done

Some mornings it is an element, a thermostat, a T&P that drips, a flue that needs a look. Some mornings the tank is sacrificed and the honest talk is replace. This sample will not pretend a website can decide that.

What the site can do: say that they look, then they talk, and that a price to “just put a new one in” without seeing the flue, the pan, the gas, or the electric is a guess. A live shop can add the sizes they stock. We do not invent inventory.

Signs it is failing

Water that never gets hot. Water that is hot for four minutes. A rumble you can hear from the hall. Rust at a drain. A T&P that weeps into a bucket you forgot was there. None of those are a diagnosis. They are reasons to call and book a look.
