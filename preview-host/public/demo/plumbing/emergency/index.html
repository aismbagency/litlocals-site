Tonight

What this sample treats as emergency

If water is running and you cannot stop it, if a line has opened in a ceiling, if sewage is coming up, or if a heater is leaking onto a floor — that is tonight. The sample shop puts those jobs on this page so a real owner can keep the split honest.

A dripping lav, a slow shower, a toilet that runs, a heater that is merely old — those are booked mornings. Putting them on the emergency page trains people to call “urgent” for everything, and then nobody believes the line when it matters.

Oak & Main is not a real crew. The pattern is: one number, a short list of what rolls after dark, and a sentence that tells them what to do with the valve while they wait.

While you wait

Shut it off if you can

Under the sink: the two angle stops. At the heater: the cold inlet. At the house: the main, often in a garage, a side yard, or a closet by the meter. If you cannot find it, say so on the call. Do not keep flushing a toilet that is already coming back.

Move rugs and paper. A drop cloth is not pride — it is the floor. If a ceiling is wet, stay out from under it. None of this is a license lecture. It is the two minutes that keep a small job from becoming a rebuild.

Then call (555) 014-2000 (sample). Street, what you see, whether water is still moving. That is the whole message.

What to expect on a night call

Someone asks if the water is off. They ask the street — in this sample, a river-wards address, which is fictional. They tell you if they can roll, and about how long. If they cannot, you still spoke to a person, not a form that emails at dawn.

On site they look, then they talk. Stop the water first. Then the repair, or a safe hold until parts. A live shop writes their after-hours fee on this strip if they charge one. This sample does not invent a price.
